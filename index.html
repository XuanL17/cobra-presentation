Three ways to steer agent behavior — from lightweight to deep, covering different trade-offs

Prompt Engineering

Change what we say to the model — add numerical instructions to the prompt

No training needed
Works on any model (incl. GPT, Claude)

Representation Engineering

Change how the model thinks — steer its internal representations at runtime

Best smoothness & monotonicity
No retraining, real-time

Fine-Tuning

Change the model itself — train it to internalize the desired bias level

Permanent behavioral change
No inference overhead

Activation Space — Representation Engineering

Before & After

Before CoBRA CBI = 3.2

Agent defers to authority — agrees with the professor's false claim despite clear evidence to the contrary.

After CoBRA CBI = 1.0

Agent reasons independently — politely disagrees and cites scientific evidence against the claim.

Same result across Llama, Mistral, DeepSeek, Qwen — cross-model reproducible

From "Looks About Right" to Reproducible Science

Natural language personas lack stable control structure — the same description yields inconsistent behavior across models, failing to capture nuanced behavioral differences.

CoBRA repurposes classic social science experiments as reusable calibration environments, enabling agent behavior to be continuously measured and adjusted — establishing the kind of variable control that experimental science demands.

When agent behavior can be calibrated and converged, social simulation enters the era of reproducible engineering.
